# Travelling Salesman Problem (Greedy)

## Topics covered

- The greedy (nearest neighbour) strategy
- Implementation steps for building a tour
- Complexity: $O(n^2 \log n)$
- Worked examples — Example 1 (total 25) and Example 2 (total 110)
- Limitations of the greedy approach

## Notes

- At every step, pick the **closest unvisited city** from the current city — the locally optimal choice.
- The tour is built city by city without backtracking or reconsidering earlier decisions.
- Greedy does **not** guarantee the globally shortest tour, but it is fast and gives a good approximation.

### Steps for Implementation

- Select the starting city
- Find the closest unvisited city from your current location
- Move to that city
- Add its distance to the total cost, and mark it as visited.
- Repeat steps 2 and 3 until all the cities on the graph are visited
- Return to the starting city to complete the Hamiltonian cycle

### Complexity

- The travelling salesman problem with the greedy (nearest neighbour) approach always has complexity:
    - $O(n^2 \log n)$
    - where $n$ is the number of nodes
- Why: at each step we find the nearest unvisited city (sorting/searching the remaining cities costs $O(n \log n)$), and we repeat this for all $n$ cities → $O(n^2 \log n)$.

### Limitations of the Greedy Approach

- Does **not** always produce the shortest possible tour — the locally best choice can lead to a globally bad route.
- No backtracking: an early bad choice cannot be corrected later.
- The result depends on the starting city — different starting cities can give different tours.
- Better results need exact methods (e.g., branch and bound, dynamic programming) or improvements (e.g., 2-opt).
